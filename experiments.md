# Agent Systems Evaluation Lab - Experiment and Decision Ledger

**Status:** hypotheses, not features. **Updated:** 2026-09-25. The [manifest](manifest.md) distinguishes the north star from the first build; this file tests what could change either.

## How to use this ledger

For each experiment, record the baseline, test setup, versions, cost, result, failure examples, uncertainty, and decision. Mark it **proposed**, **running**, **supported**, **rejected**, or **inconclusive**. Compare alternatives on the same workflow and independent outcome checks. Do not promote a capability claim on the strength of a plausible demo alone. A useful pilot is not yet evidence of product differentiation. Definitions and source boundaries are in [references.md](references.md).

When an experiment runs, save a dated record under `results/` named `E<ID>-YYYY-MM-DD.md`; include the protocol, agent/world/scenario/verifier versions, raw artifact locations, costs, counterexamples, and conclusion. Link that record from its row or the decision log. Never replace a prior result in place. Mark an external capability **documented**, **hands-on tested**, or **demonstrated here** rather than conflating them.

## Later research: the evaluator's working model

The human-evaluator analogy suggests more than retaining test results. Explore a source-linked view of users and affected parties, workflow and permission boundaries, observed use versus intended use, world-fidelity gaps, competing failure explanations, prior corrections, and untested territory. It should know when to probe, ask, defer, or stop. Keep authoritative owner decisions separate from observations and hypotheses; preserve dissenting evidence and the history of wrong guesses. This is a research agenda for E24-E25 and related drift/selection work, not a new requirement for the first pilot or a claim that an LLM understands an application's purpose from documents or traces alone.

## Near-term choices to settle

- **First workflow:** refund/order is a candidate because writes and retries have clear ground truth; compare it with adapting the existing claims environment before committing.
- **Effect capture:** start with one database or tool boundary and explicit before/after snapshots; decide later whether a [Chronicle](https://github.com/theagentplane/chronicle) contribution, plugin, or separate [OpenTelemetry](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/concepts/observability-primer/) adapter is the right package.
- **Autonomy level:** generated worlds and verifiers can be proposed automatically; determine experimentally what evidence allows automatic promotion to a release gate. Separate candidate-agent improvement from lab self-improvement.
- **Reuse boundary:** begin with plain artifact IDs, provenance, dependency versions, and a cost ledger. Cache deterministic setup selectively; do not reuse a prior candidate's pass as the verdict for a changed or stochastic candidate. Defer cross-project evidence memory and formal checks until E17 shows net value.
- **Scope boundary:** ask the first workflow owner for a minimal intent contract and safety obligations; label unsupported cases instead of discarding them. Do not let generated tests or production traces expand the charter automatically.
- **Commercial path:** first establish a useful internal/OSS artifact. Treat a startup decision as a separate experiment in buyer demand and measurable advantage, not an assumed outcome.
- **Publication:** hold the three educational drafts until E13 yields a measured result. Publish one focused piece first; credit and verify existing vendor capabilities using [references.md](references.md).
- **App understanding:** defer a broad app dossier until the basic world, oracle, and limits record work. For the pilot, obtain a short owner-approved intent contract and record unresolved questions; E24-E25 will test whether richer notes and interactive clarification add measurable value.
